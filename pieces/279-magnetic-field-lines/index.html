POLARITY — Magnetic Dipole Field Lines

Magnetic Dipole Field

A magnetic dipole with moment m at position p produces a field B at point r:

B(r) = (μ₀/4π) · [3(m·r̂)r̂ − m] / |r−p|³

where r̂ is the unit vector from the dipole to the field point. This piece uses a dimensionless constant K = 10⁵ and adds a soft-core term r²+r₀² to avoid the singularity at the dipole centre.

∇·B = 0 — No Magnetic Monopoles

Unlike electric field lines, which start on positive charges and end on negative charges, magnetic field lines form closed loops. The divergence theorem applied to ∇·B = 0 guarantees that every field line exiting a north pole must return to a south pole — there are no isolated magnetic monopoles.

Iron-Filing Texture

The classic iron-filing visualisation scatters N = N_FILINGS short segments across the canvas, each aligned to the local B direction at that point. Position seeds are fixed on resize; only their orientations are recomputed each frame as the dipoles move. Segments near the poles, where |B| is large, are coloured brighter gold; distant segments stay amber.

RK4 Streamlines

Optional long streamlines are traced with fourth-order Runge-Kutta integration along B/|B|, seeding at equal angular intervals around each dipole centre. Each streamline terminates when it exits the canvas or enters within POLE_RAD pixels of any dipole centre (completing its closed loop), or after MAX_STEPS steps.

Animation

Two dipoles slowly rotate their moment vectors and drift on sinusoidal orbits. The iron-filing orientations update every frame, so the pattern evolves continuously rather than jumping. Streamlines are retraced on every frame only when enabled.

Colour Palette

Background: charcoal #0e0e12. Field strength maps through a log scale from warm iron amber #c8871a at weak field to bright gold #f5d060 at the poles.
